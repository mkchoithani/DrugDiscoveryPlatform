The problem

Most of a drug programme is spent making molecules that do not work

A medicinal chemist can make a few hundred compounds a year. The space of plausible molecules is effectively infinite. Every wrong choice costs weeks of lab time.

What chemists need

Not a prediction of everything. A ranked shortlist of the next 24 compounds worth the bench time, and an honest statement of how much to trust it.

What the market sells

Accuracy figures from random train/test splits — the one condition that never holds in production, because tomorrow's chemistry is not a random sample of today's.

We measured that gap on our own model. On a random split it scores ρ 0.87. On the split that matches production it scores ρ 0.11 — same model, same data, same day. Both numbers are real. Only one of them is a forecast of anything.

What we built

A closed design–make–test–analyse loop

Design
Model ranks the library on 8 objectives
Make
Chemist reviews and approves the batch
Test
Assay returns measured potency
Analyse
Model retrains; the loop tightens

Running today on 9,000 real compounds from public assay data (ChEMBL), across four unrelated targets: two kinases, a GPCR and a zinc metalloenzyme.

  • Multi-parameter scoring — potency, drug-likeness, synthetic accessibility, size, diversity
  • Six selection strategies, benchmarked against each other rather than assumed
  • Every measurement stored, so the loop is reproducible from the database alone

The one result we stand behind

2.37× the base rate of finding potent compounds, on real assay data

Retrospective active learning: the library is every real compound for the target, and a measurement is revealed only when the loop chooses to make it. 10 cycles × 24 compounds = 240 made out of ~9,000.

The harness checks out. Random selection recovers each library's own base rate within noise on all four targets — a benchmark quietly rigged in the loop's favour would fail here first. The ordering is identical on all four. The compounds chosen are also more drug-like: mean QED 0.76–0.81 against 0.52–0.57 for random.

What the examiner found

The number vendors quote is the number that does not matter

Spearman ρ between prediction and measurement, same model, three ways of splitting the data.

The confidence estimate degrades the same way (mean ρ 0.44 → 0.36 → 0.12) and inverts entirely on μ-opioid: the model is most certain exactly where it is most wrong. That single finding changed the product — the default exploration parameter was cut from 1.0 to 0.25, and the tool now recommends a value from the worst split rather than the best. Three candidate fixes have now been measured and none works: docking failed four times, a graph neural net tied, and cell-painting morphology — the one that was different in kind — tied on scaffolds. That third one does not close the question: the image data carries no dates, so it could not be tested on the time split, which is the column this slide is about. We would rather say the fix is untested than imply it failed, or that one exists.

We examined ourselves too

Four of our own findings did not survive — one was shipping

2.75× → 0.71×

We re-ran our loop on PostEra's published DEL-simulator landscape instead of our own. Performance collapsed. Our simulator was roughly 5× too easy. The README now labels 2.75× an upper bound. The real-data 2.37× never used the simulator and stands.

Three verdict bugs

The code that decides pass/fail had three errors — two of them approving a method I expected to win. A verdict function encodes its author's expectations and is no safeguard against them.

A metric with no resolution

"3.77 versus 3.74" read as a difference. Converted back to molecules it was 10.0 against 9.9, out of 10 — the metric had saturated. The suite now refuses a saturated column.

A default that was shipping

Our scoring profile marked down 138 approved antibacterials 19.6 points more often than other approved drugs, CI [+10.8, +28.7]. Daptomycin scored exactly zero. Those drugs exist, so the error is ours — and it had been in every campaign since the profile was written.

What caught all four was the same discipline: turn every ratio back into a count of actual molecules, and attach a p-value. A "win" of 7 actives against 6 is p = 0.65. It is not a win.

The decisive test · resolved 25 November

Docking looked like our first win. It was the benchmark, not the physics.

On DUD-E, docking beat molecular size (AUC 0.629 vs 0.527). But DUD-E’s “inactives” were never tested in a lab, so we re-ran it on LIT-PCBA, where every inactive is a measured one.

MethodAUCEF 1% Actives in top 21P by chance
2D forest (fingerprints)0.723 6.4250.0008
docking (smina, 4QTE)0.6221.28 10.55
heavy atom count0.5770.00 01.00
pharmacophore field0.5512.57 20.18

Docking’s early enrichment is one molecule — what random selection produces 55% of the time. Its AUC edge over counting atoms is +0.045, 95% CI [−0.013, +0.099]: the interval contains zero. On DUD-E that gap was +0.102, so about half of it was decoy bias and the rest is noise. Docking is also significantly worse than the plain 2D model.

Where this test is weak, stated plainly: with 77 actives the interval cannot exclude a gap as large as DUD-E’s. “Not significant” is not “shown to be zero”. The verdict rests on the enrichment being a flat chance result rather than a near miss — and on this being the fourth independent test docking has failed.

Synthesis routes (retrosynthesis)CPU, free A route model we trust, and a chemist to accept or reject each route
Billion-scale candidate pools$50–200 per campaign An Enamine REAL or equivalent catalogue licence
Actually making molecules— A chemist who will look at the batch and say "no, not that one"
Published practitioner guidance is that batch size should match reviewer capacity, not what the algorithm can generate. Every remaining step needs human chemistry judgement, not a bigger GPU. That is the honest bottleneck.

The ask

What we want from this review

1 · A chemist

Someone who will review a proposed batch of 24 and veto compounds. Without this the loop cannot close on real chemistry, and no amount of compute substitutes.

2 · A target

One internal programme with assay data we can run the examiner against. Public data got us this far; it cannot tell us whether this works on your chemistry.

3 · Agreement on the standard

That a capability ships only when it beats a known answer, with the molecule count and the p-value shown. This is slower. It is the whole product.

We have deleted three of our own features, demoted two from "rejected" to a tie, and corrected five of our own claims. That is the evidence that the numbers we do report mean something.
